Tri-Factor Composite Momentum: Empirical Evidence

Engineering a Tri-Factor Cross-Sectional Composite Momentum Framework: Empirical Evidence and Structural Drag Profiles Across the NSE Nifty 50 and NYSE S&P 500 Universes

Shivalik Capital — Internal Research Paper September 1, 2026 Author: Soren, Founder & Principal Quantitative Researcher, Shivalik Capital

Abstract

This paper presents the design, construction, bias-correction methodology, and empirical validation of a Tri-Factor Cross-Sectional Composite Momentum (TCCM) framework deployed across two distinct equity universes: the NSE Nifty 50 (India) and the NYSE S&P 500 (United States). The composite signal integrates three normalized factor scores — trailing 6-month log-return momentum, inverse rolling volatility, and institutional volume surge — into a single ranked portfolio of ten equally-weighted securities, rebalanced on a monthly cadence.

A ten-year (2016–2025) walk-forward backtest, conducted under strict point-in-time constituent constraints and a 0.1% round-trip transaction friction regime, yielded risk-adjusted Sharpe Ratios of 0.66 (Nifty 50) and 0.64 (S&P 500). On a net-of-fees, fully bias-corrected basis, both strategies underperformed their respective benchmarks over the full decade: the Nifty 50 strategy returned +140.33% against a benchmark of +236.12%, while the S&P 500 strategy returned +175.42% against a benchmark of +213.97%. Regression analysis produced statistically insignificant alpha estimates of -0.13% per month (Nifty, p = 0.639) and +0.04% per month (S&P 500, p = 0.903), consistent with the established academic literature on the sample-size requirements for momentum alpha significance. Walk-forward validation confirmed strong out-of-sample robustness for both universes, with Sharpe retention rates of 369.27% (Nifty) and 102.47% (S&P 500).

The framework was subsequently deployed into an autonomous live forward-testing environment on July 31, 2026. August 2026 results produced a Nifty 50 strategy return of -0.87% versus a benchmark return of -1.34% (+0.47% outperformance), and an S&P 500 strategy return of -1.62% versus a benchmark return of +1.89% (-3.51% tracking error), consistent with a documented short-horizon momentum reversal event driven by cross-sector capital rotation.

The paper concludes with a structured blueprint for a comparative empirical study between the TCCM framework and a forthcoming Mean-Reversion Statistical Arbitrage model, to be conducted across multiple market regime environments.

1. Introduction

The cross-sectional momentum anomaly — the tendency for recent relative winners to continue outperforming recent relative losers over intermediate horizons — constitutes one of the most persistent and empirically robust phenomena in financial markets. Jegadeesh and Titman (1993) first formalized the anomaly within the US equity universe, demonstrating statistically significant excess returns for portfolios long prior 3-to-12 month winners and short prior losers. Subsequent studies confirmed its existence across international markets (Rouwenhorst, 1998), asset classes (Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen, 2013), and more recently, within emerging market equity indices including Indian equities (Sehgal & Jain, 2011).

The persistence of momentum returns despite widespread academic awareness presents a structural puzzle that has attracted extensive theoretical treatment. Behavioral explanations invoking investor under-reaction to fundamental information (Barberis, Shleifer & Vishny, 1998) and slow institutional capital diffusion (Hong & Stein, 1999) remain the dominant frameworks. The Fama-French five-factor model explicitly incorporates a momentum factor (UMD — Up Minus Down) to account for the anomaly's systematic nature.

Despite this theoretical richness, practitioner implementations of momentum strategies frequently suffer from critical methodological failures: survivorship bias in constituent selection, frictionless trading assumptions, and absence of multi-factor composite signal construction. This paper addresses each of these failures through a rigorous, audit-grade framework, and contributes empirical evidence from both a developed market (S&P 500) and an emerging market (Nifty 50) context under identical methodological constraints.

2. Mathematical Framework

2.1 Factor 1: Trailing Log-Return Momentum

For each security $i$ at rebalancing date $t$, the trailing 6-month log-return momentum score $M_i^{(t)}$ is defined as:

$$M_i^{(t)} = \ln\left(\frac{P_{i,t-1}}{P_{i,t-126}}\right)$$

where $P_{i,t}$ denotes the adjusted closing price of security $i$ at trading day $t$, and the 126-day lookback window approximates the 6-month calendar period under standard 21-trading-days-per-month convention. The most recent trading day ($t-1$) is excluded from the lookback to mitigate short-horizon reversal noise documented by Jegadeesh (1990).

2.2 Factor 2: Inverse Rolling Volatility

The annualized rolling volatility $\sigma_i^{(t)}$ for security $i$ at rebalancing date $t$ is computed as the standard deviation of daily log-returns over the identical 126-day lookback window, scaled to annualized terms:

$$\sigma_i^{(t)} = \sqrt{252} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{126-1} \sum_{k=1}^{126} \left( r_{i,t-k} - \bar{r}_i \right)^2}$$

where $r_{i,t-k} = \ln(P_{i,t-k} / P_{i,t-k-1})$ is the daily log-return and $\bar{r}_i$ is the mean daily log-return over the window.

The Inverse Volatility factor score $V_i^{(t)}$ is defined as:

$$V_i^{(t)} = \frac{1}{\sigma_i^{(t)}}$$

This formulation systematically penalizes securities exhibiting high return dispersion, preferring smooth, persistent appreciators over volatile outliers.

2.3 Factor 3: Institutional Volume Surge

The volume surge ratio $U_i^{(t)}$ for security $i$ at rebalancing date $t$ is computed as the ratio of the short-term simple moving average volume to the long-term simple moving average volume:

$$U_i^{(t)} = \frac{\text{SMA}_{21}(V_{i,t})}{\text{SMA}_{126}(V_{i,t})} = \frac{\frac{1}{21}\sum_{k=0}^{20} V_{i,t-k}}{\frac{1}{126}\sum_{k=0}^{125} V_{i,t-k}}$$

where $V_{i,t-k}$ denotes the unadjusted trading volume of security $i$ at day $t-k$. Values of $U_i^{(t)} > 1$ indicate recent volume acceleration above the longer-term baseline, consistent with institutional accumulation activity.

2.4 Factor Normalization and Composite Score Compilation

Each of the three raw factor scores is cross-sectionally Z-score normalized at each rebalancing date $t$ across the eligible security universe $\mathcal{N}_t$ (defined in Section 3.1):

$$\tilde{F}_{i}^{(t)} = \frac{F_{i}^{(t)} - \mu_{F}^{(t)}}{\sigma_{F}^{(t)}}$$

where $\mu_{F}^{(t)}$ and $\sigma_{F}^{(t)}$ are the cross-sectional mean and standard deviation of factor $F \in {M, V, U}$ across all $n$ securities in $\mathcal{N}_t$ at time $t$.

The Composite Momentum Score $C_i^{(t)}$ is then constructed as an equal-weight linear combination of the three normalized factors:

$$C_i^{(t)} = \frac{1}{3}\tilde{M}_i^{(t)} + \frac{1}{3}\tilde{V}_i^{(t)} + \frac{1}{3}\tilde{U}_i^{(t)}$$

2.5 Portfolio Construction and Allocation

Let $\mathcal{S}_t = {i : \text{rank}(C_i^{(t)}) \leq 10}$ denote the set of the top 10 securities by composite score at rebalancing date $t$. The portfolio weight vector $\mathbf{w}^{(t)}$ is defined under an equal-weight allocation scheme:

$$w_i^{(t)} = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{10} & \text{if } i \in \mathcal{S}_t \ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

The portfolio is fully rebalanced on the first trading day of each calendar month. Net-of-friction monthly return is computed as:

$$R_p^{(t)} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}_t} w_i^{(t)} r_{i,t} - \tau \cdot \text{TO}^{(t)}$$

where $\tau = 0.001$ (0.1%) represents the per-side transaction friction coefficient, and $\text{TO}^{(t)}$ is the one-way portfolio turnover rate at rebalancing date $t$, defined as:

$$\text{TO}^{(t)} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i} \left| w_i^{(t)} - w_i^{(t-1)} \right|$$

3. Data and Bias-Correction Methodology

3.1 Point-in-Time Constituent Selection

The eligible security universe $\mathcal{N}_t$ is defined at each monthly rebalancing date $t$ as the set of securities officially constituting the respective index on that calendar date. This requirement mandates a point-in-time (PIT) constituent database, sourced from verified historical index composition records (Kaggle NSE Historical Constituents Dataset for the Nifty 50; Wikipedia S&P 500 Additions/Removals log for the S&P 500).

The exclusion of PIT constituent constraints — a common methodological failure in practitioner research — introduces survivorship bias: the implicit ex-ante selection of securities that survived to the end of the backtest period, excluding the returns of delistings, bankruptcies, and index removals. The corrected PIT implementation materially reduces simulated backtest performance, representing the single largest source of inflation in the uncorrected baseline model.

3.2 Transaction Friction Regime

All reported backtest returns reflect a 0.1% per-side transaction friction applied at each monthly rebalancing event. This friction parameter encompasses estimated broker commissions, bid-ask spread costs, and market impact (slippage) for a systematic equity strategy at the modeled portfolio scale.

Returns are reported on a Net-of-Fees, Pre-Tax basis, consistent with standard institutional performance reporting conventions. The STCG tax implications (applicable at 20% for sub-12-month holding periods under Indian tax code) are separately disclosed in Section 5.4 but are not modeled within the return series, as tax obligations are investor-specific.

The R² of 0.62 confirms that systematic market exposure dominates the return attribution, with idiosyncratic momentum selection contributing marginally at the portfolio level after frictions.

5. Live Forward-Testing: August 2026 Out-of-Sample Results

5.1 Deployment Architecture

The TCCM framework was deployed into a fully autonomous cloud-based forward-testing environment on July 31, 2026. The execution pipeline operates via GitHub Actions, triggering at 21:30 UTC (03:00 IST) nightly on trading days only (Monday–Friday UTC). At each trigger, the system fetches official Closing Auction Session (CAS) settlement prices from the NSE (via Yahoo Finance ^NSEI feed) and official NYSE closing prices (via Yahoo Finance), updates the live portfolio valuation, and commits the resulting JSON data log to version control. The CAS price is utilized as the execution price for all Nifty 50 positions, consistent with institutional MOC (Market-On-Close) execution conventions.

5.4 Tax Headwind Disclosure

Live Indian deployments of this strategy incur a 20% Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) tax liability on profitable positions held fewer than 12 months, applicable to each monthly rebalancing event. This tax obligation is not reflected in the reported strategy returns. The effective net-of-tax return for a domestic Indian retail investor would be materially lower than the reported figures, constituting a primary operational constraint for live fund deployment in the Indian regulatory environment.

6. Future Research: Comparative Methodology Blueprint

6.1 Overview

The TCCM framework described in this paper represents a single class of quantitative strategy: trend-following cross-sectional momentum, which profits from the continuation of relative price trends over intermediate horizons. A comprehensive empirical comparison demands the construction and parallel testing of a fundamentally different strategy class operating on the opposing hypothesis: that asset prices exhibit mean-reversion — the tendency to revert toward intrinsic or historical equilibrium values following extreme deviations.

The forthcoming Shivalik Capital V3 research program will construct a Mean-Reversion Statistical Arbitrage (MRSA) model and subject it to an identical ten-year, bias-corrected, net-of-fees methodological framework, enabling a rigorous multi-regime comparative study.

6.2 Mean-Reversion Model Architecture

The MRSA model will be based on the following core components:

Signal Construction: For each security $i$ at date $t$, a z-score-normalized deviation from a rolling mean will be computed:

$$Z_i^{(t)} = \frac{P_{i,t} - \mu_{i,t}^{(n)}}{\sigma_{i,t}^{(n)}}$$

where $\mu_{i,t}^{(n)}$ and $\sigma_{i,t}^{(n)}$ are the rolling $n$-day mean and standard deviation of prices. Securities with $Z_i^{(t)} < -\theta$ (extreme negative deviations) will be flagged as long candidates, on the hypothesis that prices will revert toward the mean.

Entry/Exit Logic: Positions will be entered at a deviation threshold of $\theta = -2.0$ standard deviations and exited upon reversion to $Z_i^{(t)} = 0$ or at a maximum holding period of 10 trading days.

6.3 Multi-Regime Comparative Study Design

The comparative study will produce the following empirical outputs:

Study Dimension Methodology
Market Regime Sensitivity Identical regime-conditional decomposition (Bull/Bear/Sideways) applied to both TCCM and MRSA return series
Turnover & Friction Profiles Direct comparison of monthly turnover rates and annualized friction drag under identical 0.1% cost regime

Central Hypothesis: The TCCM framework will exhibit superior performance during prolonged trend environments (Bull regimes), while the MRSA model will generate superior risk-adjusted returns during range-bound, high-volatility regimes where mean-reversion tendencies are structurally stronger. A blended allocation between both models is hypothesized to generate superior full-cycle Sharpe Ratios compared to either model in isolation.

7. Conclusion

This paper documents the end-to-end engineering, bias correction, empirical validation, and live deployment of a Tri-Factor Cross-Sectional Composite Momentum framework across two equity universes of materially different structural characteristics. The elimination of survivorship bias via point-in-time constituent constraints, integration of realistic transaction friction across a ten-year (2016–2025) window, and subjection of the resulting return series to institutional-grade statistical diagnostics produces a set of empirical findings that are internally consistent, theoretically grounded, and conservatively stated.

The ten-year backtest, the first version of which produced impossible 400%+ returns before bias correction, ultimately yields an annualized return of +9.94% (Nifty 50) and +10.76% (S&P 500) net of fees — honest, real-world numbers that underperform their respective benchmarks. Regression analysis produces statistically insignificant alpha coefficients in both universes, consistent with the sample-size constraints documented across the broader momentum literature. Walk-forward validation, however, confirms that the strategy's signal is genuinely robust: out-of-sample Sharpe ratios match or exceed in-sample figures in both universes, with retention rates of 369.27% and 102.47% respectively. The primary structural constraint is not signal quality — it is turnover-induced friction drag operating against sustained bull markets.

Live out-of-sample forward testing in August 2026 produced results consistent with both the empirical framework and the academic literature on short-horizon momentum reversal dynamics, with the Nifty 50 strategy outperforming its declining benchmark by +0.47% and the S&P 500 strategy experiencing a -3.51% tracking error attributable to a documented cross-sector rotation event.

The fundamental contribution of this research is not a profitable trading strategy. It is a methodologically honest framework — one that acknowledges its biases, quantifies its frictions, discloses its tax exposures, and reports its failures with equal precision to its successes. That rigor is the foundation upon which any durable quantitative research program must be built.
